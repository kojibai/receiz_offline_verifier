# Receiz v120.0.0 — Living Proof Subjects

Release date: 2026-08-16

Developer surface: `@receiz/sdk@120.0.0`. Durable proof-memory law: first admission only, then append forever.

V120 makes creatures, characters, vehicles, agents, collectibles, and other proof-native beings first-class Receiz subjects. One immutable subject identity carries exact namespace bytes, ownership, complete proof history, a portable mind, event-derived memory, relationships, inventory, mandates, and deterministic world participation across applications, devices, owners, and Twin providers.

- `@receiz/sdk`, `@receiz/mcp-server`, `@receiz/ai-skills`, the application contract, schemas, registry, emulator, Receiz Docs, and constitutional verifier share the exact `120.0.0` identity.
- A subject Twin follows `canonical head -> entire proof history -> indexes -> relevant exact primary objects -> reasoning -> cited response`; the bounded 96-object working set never truncates canonical history.
- Kai/Merkle/Fibonacci proof-brain commitments and streamed segment resolution keep the head compact and retrieval bounded at million-object scale.
- Model speech, intentions, emotions, animation, and audio cues are non-authoritative. Only a validated typed command or atomic transaction can admit a world event.
- Factual memory is rebuilt from admitted event IDs. AI summaries remain derived projections.
- Relationships, battles, gifts, trades, and other multi-subject effects advance every affected head atomically or write nothing.
- Digest-bound mandates are reverified at execution time and bound action, region, interaction, privacy, battle, inventory, value, frequency, provider, safety, application, tenant, expiry, and revocation.
- Bearer transfer supports known-recipient and open handoff, pending custody, offline inspection, one-time claim, cancellation, exact-head replay protection, inventory disposition, privacy policy, immediate former-owner revocation, and a newly sealed artifact preserving the same subject identity.
- The profile release projection carries all 469 complete Showcase proof objects. Its sealed composite head advances once for each admitted object and reopens without rediscovering already-held history.
- The Twin proof brain indexes all complete Showcase objects and full long-form text at the exact composite head.
- Long-form narration verifies the enclosing proof object first, preserves an anchor carried only in `payload_b64`, and exposes Play only for a ready derivative bound to the exact text digest.
- Published PDF proof objects remain viewable and downloadable as their exact `.receized.pdf` bytes; incidental Identity Seal prose cannot reclassify a document as PBI-gated Identity Artwork.
- Seven new v120 AI Skills and 37 typed living-subject MCP tools expose SDK parity without granting MCP or model authority.

## The release narrative

Before v120, an application could represent proof-native creatures, but it still had to invent a second authority system for creature identity, mind, autonomy, social state, trade, battle, and transfer. A person-scoped Twin could simulate a creature through a thread key, but that did not give the creature its own portable identity, complete proof memory, bounded mandate, or atomically replayable world history.

V120 closes that boundary. A living subject is now a proof object with an immutable identity and append-only life. Its Twin can speak as that subject from the exact proof history. Its model may suggest what it wants to do, but the deterministic Receiz world runtime alone decides what happened. Its factual memories cite admitted events. Its relationships and inventory change atomically. Its owner may authorize bounded autonomy once, revoke it immediately, or transfer the same subject to another owner without rewriting identity or dropping history.

The result is one authority system for the complete Wildz lifecycle: proof object, living mind, world action, social continuity, ownership, transfer, replay, cross-application continuity, and independently verifiable provenance.

## 1. Immutable living-subject identity

`receiz.subject.v1` introduces a generic proof-native subject. `subjectId`, `proofObjectId`, `subjectType`, `ownerReceizId`, `identityDigest`, `genesisDigest`, and `createdAtKai` are immutable. Current owner, application, device, and provider are append-only state beneath that identity.

Every artifact carries `identity`, `canonicalState`, `selfModel`, `memory`, `relationships`, `inventory`, `abilities`, `condition`, `worldPosition`, `mandates`, `eventHistory`, `projections`, and `applicationNamespaces`. Unknown namespaces survive append, export, import, transfer, and reconciliation byte-for-byte. A new application can understand more of the artifact without deleting what an earlier application carried.

## 2. A complete streamed proof brain

The subject brain is not a large prompt and it is not a summary database. The canonical head commits the entire content-addressed history. Kai ordering, Merkle membership, Fibonacci skip commitments, and segmented indexes locate relevant objects. Retrieval then resolves and verifies the exact primary proof-object bytes before reasoning.

```text
canonical head
-> entire proof history
-> Kai/Merkle/Fibonacci indexes
-> relevant proof references
-> exact primary proof-object bytes
-> digest and head verification
-> reasoning
-> answer with provenance
```

The 96-object value remains an excellent bounded reasoning window; it is no longer mistaken for the brain’s total memory. The index may address millions of proof objects while one answer reads only the exact relevant primary objects. Full long-form posts remain primary bytes, not abbreviated canonical memory. Excerpts, summaries, embeddings, and model context are locators and derived projections only.

Proof retrieval streams head, candidates, exact objects, passages, and the final context receipt so useful work can begin without waiting for the complete candidate set. Missing or mismatched primary bytes fail closed.

## 3. Subject Twin and portable mind

`receiz.subject.mind.v1` and `receiz.subject.self_model.v1` give each subject its own Twin and portable brain, independent of its owner’s personal Twin. Twin responses distinguish spoken response, proposed intentions, observed facts, proof-derived memories, and non-authoritative emotional/performance cues.

The portable mind binds exact subject identity, prior subject and mind heads, history head, memory visibility, and unknown namespaces. It verifies offline, paints immediately from held truth, preserves forks instead of replacing them, and moves between devices, applications, and providers without rewriting the subject.

## 4. AI proposes; the world decides

`receiz.subject.intent.v1` records structured speech and proposed intentions with target IDs, reason, confidence, required capabilities, observed head, memory citations, and optional performance. Provider, model, version, prompt digest, context digest, and output digest support reproducibility.

None of those fields is proof authority. A model statement never becomes a world event because the model said it occurred.

Every consequential action follows one exact sequence:

```text
propose intent
-> construct typed command
-> validate current subject and world heads
-> verify owner capability or current bounded mandate
-> decide with the deterministic reducer
-> atomically append event
-> return independently verifiable receipt
-> rebuild projections
```

Rejected decisions advance no head, write no event, update no projection, and allocate no Kai coordinate. Retries return the original admission identity and cannot duplicate an event.

## 5. Atomic multi-subject reality

Meetings, mutual relationships, battles, gifts, and trades affect more than one history. `receiz.world.transaction.v1` stages every affected subject and world head against exact expectations, verifies participant authority, and admits all effects together.

There is no one-sided friendship, trade, battle, or inventory result. A conflict or stale head has one deterministic response and zero writes. The receipt is independently verifiable by every participant.

## 6. Event-derived memory, relationships, and inventory

Factual memory is an event-derived projection. Conversation, episodic world, relationship, battle, discovery, ownership/care, and inventory/trade memories cite admitted event IDs. A compact AI summary may help retrieval, but the projection can always be rebuilt from the exact head.

Relationship edges are mutual and consent-aware. Familiarity, trust, rivalry, affection, mentorship, visibility, interaction policy, and owner blocklists change only through admitted events. Inventory use and trade bind exact ownership heads and mandate/value limits. A trade outside autonomy requires owner approval; an admitted swap changes both owners atomically.

## 7. Bounded autonomy and durable execution

An owner activates one exact mandate digest. The mandate bounds actions, regions, creatures and owners, conversation/privacy, battle, item use/gifts/trades, value exposure, approval thresholds, frequency, daily limits, memory visibility, expiry, providers, safety, application, and tenant.

The runtime uses durable scheduling, leases, crash recovery, deterministic Kai clocks, bounded offline catch-up, budgets, stale-head rejection, and execution-time mandate verification. A pause, revocation, expiration, ownership change, provider mismatch, timeout, or malformed intent stops admission. It never invents an event to hide a model or runtime failure.

## 8. Bearer ownership transfer without identity loss

Bearer transfer supports a known recipient or an open handoff. A sealed one-time instrument binds the exact current subject and ownership heads, recipient policy, expiry, pending custody, inventory disposition, prior-owner conversation policy, registry, reducer, and claim capability.

Pending custody blocks conflicting sale, trade, or transfer. Claim independently verifies the exact instrument bytes, recipient capability, current heads, expiry, cancellation, and replay state before one atomic ownership transition. The same identity, genesis, complete history, public memory, relationships, provenance, transferred inventory, and unknown namespaces continue into a newly sealed artifact.

The same transition revokes the former owner’s mandates, private Twin access, queued autonomous jobs, and delegated capabilities. Autonomy remains paused until the new owner explicitly authorizes a new mandate. Failed, stale, expired, cancelled, or replayed claims change nothing.

## 9. Cross-application and live continuity

Subject and world additions expose replay cursors, causal parents, SSE, signed webhook delivery, duplicate tolerance, partition recovery, and deterministic convergence. A reconnect resolves from an exact prior head; it never uses “latest snapshot wins.” Structural conflicts preserve forks for explicit proof-native reconciliation.

Moving a subject between Receiz applications changes application permissions, not identity or prior history. Owning the subject does not automatically disclose private memory to every application; delegated scopes remain narrow.

## 10. Voice, narration, and live performance

`streamPerformance` emits `reply_delta`, `audio_chunk`, `viseme`, `gaze`, `blink`, `breath`, `emotion`, `gesture`, `intent_proposed`, and `reply_done`. Timing, confidence, source-state/context digests, and reduced-motion compatibility make the stream reproducible and usable while keeping it non-canonical.

Long-form narration uses the same primary-object law. The enclosing proof object is verified first; a legacy anchor carried only inside `payload_b64` is preserved for access evaluation; the exact long-form digest binds the derivative. A Play control appears only when that exact derivative is already ready. Narration can fail without changing the reader, subject, proof history, or world state.

## 11. Showcase continuity and first paint

The Showcase is one append-only sealed composite proof object. Each newly admitted proof object creates one successor head. Objects already carried by that composite are durable proof memory and must not be rediscovered on every visit.

The v120 release projection carries all 469 complete Public/Signed proof objects at its exact head. The visible presentation remains bounded for speed, but authority is complete. The top profile total and Showcase total derive from the same admitted composite count; an equal profile statistic cannot suppress a newer Showcase coordinate. Publishing succeeds only after the successor composite head settles.

Document identity follows the sealed artifact, not words inside its claim. Identity Artwork download protection binds reserved artifact filenames and image media evidence. A PDF that discusses Identity Seal remains a PDF proof object, keeps its public collection policy, and returns its exact PDF bytes instead of a JSON denial or unrelated PBI ceremony.

## 12. SDK, MCP, and AI Skills parity

`@receiz/sdk@120.0.0` exposes subject, brain, Twin, memory, relationship, inventory, trade, mandate, runtime, world command/transaction, replay, subscription, and bearer APIs.

`@receiz/mcp-server@120.0.0` adds 37 typed living-subject tools alongside the nine enclosing-artifact tools. Every result names its source primitive and registry/reducer digests. Existing app-building, command, replay, sync, reconciliation, portability, authority, MCP, testing, release, constitutional, and offline skills carry the v120 contracts and examples.

## 13. Emulator, schemas, and conformance

The SDK ships Draft 2020-12 schemas at `@receiz/sdk/schemas/v120/living-subjects`. The 19-check living-subject suite proves the ten core laws and nine bearer-transfer laws: no model-authorized events; cited factual memory; atomic multi-subject effects; retry idempotency; revocation; exact offline restoration; replay equivalence; SDK/MCP projection parity; unknown-namespace survival; canonical isolation from conversation/performance failure; double-claim rejection; stale/cancelled/expired rejection; former-owner exclusion; queued-mandate cancellation; identity/history continuity; zero-write failed transfer; and offline-claim head convergence.

## Why it matters

Wildz can use Receiz itself as the only authority system. A living subject can speak with continuity across its complete verified history while every consequential action remains deterministic, atomic, replayable, transferable, revocable, privacy-bounded, and independently verifiable.

This is not a model pretending to remember a character and it is not a database row carrying the latest character snapshot. It is a proof-native being whose identity, mind, memory, agency, social state, possessions, and ownership remain continuous through time.
